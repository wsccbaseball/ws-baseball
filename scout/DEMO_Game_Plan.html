WSCC vs Riverside CC

Series Keynote
1Who they are: a 34–17 grind-it-out, on-base machine (.454 team OBP) behind a wild, homer-prone staff (6.85 ERA, 264 BB, 54 HBP).
2What they do well: get on base (295 BB, 114 HBP), put it in play, rarely strike out. Their lineup will pressure us and clog the bases — and they'll take the extra base when it's there (27 SB).
3What we take advantage of: the talent gap is ours. Their staff is wild and homer-prone, and we out-power them 154 HR to 40 and out-run them 113 to 27. This is a game we win on ability.
4Our focus: at the plate, make them throw strikes and punish the fastball; on the mound, spin them down and take away the bases.
Make 'em throw strikes. Hammer the heater. Don't give away 90 feet.
When We Hit

This whole staff leaks fastballs and walks. The lever changes by arm — starters carry the series, so attack them first; the late-inning arms are beatable on a stack. Note our shape: we're a right-handed-heavy lineup, so the stack-LHH arms (below) put a premium on our two lefty bats — Blair (L, 26 HR) and Walker (L) — late, while the reverse-split arm is a gift for our righties.

Run On Their Catchers
Riverside CC's catchers throw poorly — opponents ran 62-for-68 (91%) against them on the year. With our 113 SB, the green light is on: get into scoring position and pressure them. Their staff's slow, walk-heavy tempo only helps us run.
When We Pitch
Pitch to the Out — their lineup is one read
The recurring RIV bat is elite OBP + fastball-handling + spin-vulnerable (Avery, Grant, Foster, Bennett all fit the mold). Collective plan: spin them down, keep the fastball out of the zone. They won't chase much and they don't strike out — so beat them in the zone with breaking stuff, and live with the walk rather than grooving a heater they'll square. They'll still reach; that's where the next two boxes matter.
Control Their Run Game — From the Mound
RIV's offense runs only at a moderate clip — 27 SB, ~82% (Carter, Foster, Grant the main movers; Avery runs into outs). The steal isn't their identity — the walk and the single are. Still, vary times, slide-step, hold the lead runner so their on-base volume doesn't become free 90 feet. Take away the easy bag and they have to string hits together against our stuff.
Setting Up the Double Play
The DP is available, especially behind the ground-ball bats — 19 GIDP as a team, led by Dawson (5; a genuine 63%-grounder) and Avery (3). When they reach — and they will — pitch down and trust the infield to turn two and erase the walks.
Watch Names — Don't Give In
The bats that beat you on a mistake — pitch around the barrel, take the walk over the damage:
  • Avery (#6) — .590 OBP, no out-by-type. You can't "get" him; execute down/away and live with the walk. Never groove him.
  • Bennett (#17, switch) — .508 SLG, barrels the fastball (3 HR, 7% miss on it). Don't give him one to handle — spin down.
  • Grant (#5) — .483 OBP / .496 SLG, sits on and squares the fastball. Bury the curve and change.
  • Keller (#24) — .514 SLG, 4 HR; lifts mistakes (FB/SL). Lower in the order, but don't exhale — the changeup is the out.

Bottom line: we have more power and more speed than they do, against a staff that hands out free bases and leaves fastballs over the plate. Our job is patience at the plate (let them walk us), damage on the heater, aggression on the bases against weak-throwing catchers — and on the mound, spin them down, hold their runners, and turn the double play. Don't try to out-grind a grind-it-out team; out-slug and out-run them.
